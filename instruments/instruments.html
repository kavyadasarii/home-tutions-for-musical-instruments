What's included, no matter which you pick

The instrument changes the sound — the way we teach it doesn't. Every plan at Raga House includes the same four things.

Teacher comes to you

No commute, no traffic — your teacher arrives at your home each week, with a spare instrument for the first lesson where needed.

Matched to your child

We pair every student with a teacher suited to their age, level and the tradition — Carnatic or Western — they want to learn.

Flexible weekly scheduling

Lessons are booked around your family's routine, and can be rescheduled with notice when exams or travel get in the way.

A practice note every visit

Your teacher leaves a short, clear note after each lesson — what to practice, how long, and what's next — so progress is easy to track.

Questions

Instruments, answered

Which instrument should my child start with?

For younger children, piano or tabla tend to be the easiest starting points since they don't require exact pitch control right away. Guitar and violin work well from around age 7, especially if a child already shows interest in a specific sound.

Do we need to own the instrument before starting?

No — for the first lesson, your teacher brings a spare instrument (guitar, violin or tabla). For piano, you'll need access to a keyboard or upright at home. We'll recommend when and what to buy once your child has settled in.

Can a student switch instruments later?

Yes, anytime. Many students try one instrument for a term and switch — your plan carries over, and we simply match you with a teacher for the new instrument.

Is the first lesson different from a regular lesson?

Not really — it's a genuine first lesson, not a sales demo. The teacher assesses your child's starting point and gets them playing something within the hour.

Can two instruments be taught in the same household?

Yes. Siblings can each learn a different instrument, or one student can add a second instrument to their plan — see the Pricing page for add-on rates.
